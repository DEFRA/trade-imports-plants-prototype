# Language

The GOV.UK style guide essentials for words on a page. Words live in the copy
files beside each page (`copy/copy.en.js` for English, `copy/copy.cy.js` for
Welsh). The `change-the-words` skill changes them for you.

## Plain English

- Use everyday words: "use", not "utilise"; "help", not "facilitate"; "let",
  not "empower"; "website" or "service", not "portal".
- Keep sentences under 25 words.
- Put the most important words first.
- Avoid "quick", "easy" and "simple": they upset people who find it hard.

## Voice

- Use the active voice: "We check your notification", not "Your notification
  is checked".
- Speak to the user as "you". Use "they" when you do not know someone's
  gender.
- Be direct and polite. Do not use "please" or "please note". Say "sorry"
  only for a serious failure, never in an error message. Never use humour.

## Capitals

- Sentence case everywhere: headings, buttons, labels and links.
- Capitals only for names, places, departments, titles with names and named
  schemes.

## Contractions

- Positive ones are fine: "you're", "we'll".
- Avoid negative ones: "cannot", not "can't"; "do not", not "don't".

## Numbers, dates and times

- Numerals for numbers, including 1 to 9 in a service ("5 notifications").
  Write "one" when it reads more naturally.
- Commas in numbers over 999: 9,000.
- Dates: 27 September 2026, with no comma and no "th". Ranges use "to": 10
  November to 21 December.
- Times: 5:30pm, 10am to 11am, "midday" and "midnight".
- Money: £75 or £75.50.

## Abbreviations

- Write out an abbreviation the first time, unless it is well known (UK, EU,
  NHS, PDF).
- No full stops in abbreviations: UK, not U.K.

## Buttons and links

- Buttons say what happens: "Save and continue", "Submit notification".
- Links say where they go: "View your notification".
- Say "select", not "click".

## Error messages

- Say how to fix it: "Enter the date the consignment will arrive".
- Use the same pattern each time: "Enter…" for a missing text answer,
  "Select…" for a missing choice, "…must be…" for a wrong format.
- Keep it short, and do not blame the user.

## Welsh

Every English string has a Welsh partner in `copy.cy.js`. When you do not have
the Welsh yet, the Welsh is written as `[Welsh needed]` followed by the
English. The hand-off brief lists every one of these for translation.
